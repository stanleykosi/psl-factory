SCR-07 · PROJECT JOURNEY · HIERARCHY AND 4 CONCEPTS · 22 NOV 2026

What a project page should say first

First the order of importance, then four ways to lay the Journey tab out in that order. Each concept shows the same project with the same facts; they differ in what leads and how much the agent does.

RANKED · MOST TO LEAST IMPORTANT
1Does anything need a person now?Held, stopped and waiting work, who it waits on, since when, and the one action. This is why most people open a project.PRIMARY
2What happens nextThe next step and the next milestone, and who moves it.PRIMARY
3Tola’s readOne plain-language summary, with how many records it read and when. Saves reading the list.SECONDARY
4Contract and money progressStages, invoices verified, paid. Kept separate (PMO completes milestones, Finance handles invoices and receipts). Unknown says unknown, quietly, once.SECONDARY
5The journeyEvery piece of work, oldest first. Reference: open when you need the detail.TERTIARY
6Who and where fromPM, client, route, the RFP it came from.TERTIARY
7The other tabsDelivery, Milestones, Documents, Money, Registers, Actions. Always there, never competing.FRAME
WHY TODAY’S PAGE FEELS ALL OVER THE PLACE
  • Six facts in one strip carry equal weight; when they are unknown, six orange “Unknown” cells shout louder than anything else.
  • Nothing says what needs a person: the held invoice is the last row of the list.
  • “What is next” sits at the bottom of the list, and Tola’s summary is in a side box most people skip.
  • The PM and route line is small grey text between the tabs and the facts, where it reads as a heading.

Same example in every concept: Lagoon Health · patient app (P-0101), seen by the PMO Lead. The breadcrumb, title and tabs stay as they are (D-170).

CONCEPT A

Status first

A one-line verdict and the next milestone lead, then a “Needs attention” list with who each item waits on, since when, and one action. Tola’s read and a quiet progress row follow; the journey is last and shows open items, with the done ones a click away.

Answers “does this need me?” in one glance. The journey becomes secondary, which suits leads more than the PM who works in it daily.

CONCEPT B

Three lanes

The work laid out by who owns it: Presale, Delivery (PMO completes milestones) and Finance (invoices and receipts). The held item gets a strip on top; what is next and Tola’s read sit under the lanes.

Makes the split between delivery and money visible, and where it is stuck. Long projects make tall lanes; the order in time is weaker.

CONCEPT D

Tola runs the project (agentic)

Tola leads with a briefing written from the records, with when and what it read. Below it, Tola’s proposals, each with its reason and evidence, for a person to send, open or dismiss; what Tola is doing right now; and one feed of what people and Tola did. Facts sit to the side as the facts Tola used.

Most action per screen and the most agentic. Depends on good proposals; every action still needs a person’s confirmation and the record to show it, and the full journey is one click away.
