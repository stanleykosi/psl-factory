SCR-07 · PROJECT JOURNEY · CONCEPT D, TAKEN FURTHER · 4 CONCEPTS · 22 NOV 2026

An agent-led project page, done properly

Concept D was the right direction. Below: references from products that already ship agent work to people, where D falls short, and four ways to take it further. Same project and facts as before (Lagoon Health, seen by the PMO Lead).

REFERENCES
Ramp · Policy Agent and agent trust

Suggestions grouped into “ready” and “needs review” queues; a required “why” linked to the exact rule; no confidence percentages, “unsure” is its own calm category; autonomy grows by rule.

Smashing · practical UX patterns for agentic AI

Intent preview (a numbered plan with Proceed, Edit, Handle it myself); an autonomy dial per kind of action; an audit timeline with time-limited undo; escalation as a question, not an error.

Linear · agent-assisted project updates

The agent drafts the project update from issues and discussions; a person posts it.

Asana · AI teammates

Work assigned to people and agents in one list; agents stop at human checkpoints; you can see what each agent is doing.

Telerik · human–agent collaboration patterns

Show the specific action and its evidence, not a generic “approve?”; ask for approval where actions are hard to undo.

WHERE CONCEPT D FALLS SHORT
  • The briefing is a large blue box whose bullets repeat the three proposal cards below it.
  • All proposals look the same weight; nothing separates “ready to send” from “Tola isn’t sure”.
  • You can’t see what happens when you press a button before you press it.
  • Evidence is a grey line, not a link to the record.
  • It never says what Tola may do on its own and what always needs a person.
  • The feed and the facts compete at the bottom, and the journey is hidden.
WHAT THE REFERENCES ADD
  • Lead with one sentence from Tola, with when and what it read; never a wall of text.
  • Group what Tola proposes: ready for you, and needs your judgment.
  • Every proposal says why and links its evidence; uncertainty is a calm label, not a score.
  • Show what will happen before you press (the plan), and offer “I’ll handle it”.
  • Say what Tola may do alone, what a person sends, and what Tola never does (money, approvals, completing milestones).
  • Keep an audit of what Tola did, with undo where it can be undone.
CONCEPT D1 · FROM RAMP, SMASHING

Decision queue

One line from Tola, then its proposals grouped into “Ready for you to send” and “Needs your judgment”. Each row: what, why, the records it is based on, and the suggested action. Opening a row shows what will happen, step by step, with Send, Edit the message, or I’ll handle it. Facts and what Tola handled today sit in a rail.

Fastest way to clear what needs you, with the evidence one glance away. The journey becomes a link.

CONCEPT D2 · FROM SMASHING INTENT PREVIEW

Tola’s plan to the next milestone

Tola lays out the steps to UAT sign-off, who owns each one (Tola, the client, Tunde, a CEO, Finance) and where it stands. The blocked step carries its own button. You confirm the plan, change it, or run it yourself.

Best at showing how the project actually gets to the next milestone, and who is in the way. Less suited to many unrelated small items.

CONCEPT D3 · FROM ASANA, RAMP AUTONOMY

People and Tola on one board

Four columns: needs you, Tola is doing, waiting on people, done this week. Tola’s cards and people’s cards look alike, with who on each. Underneath: what Tola may do on this project (reads, drafts, nudges with undo; a person sends anything to a client; never money, approvals or completing milestones).

Makes the agent a visible member of the team and states its limits on the page. A board takes more room than a list.

CONCEPT D4 · FROM LINEAR AGENT UPDATES

Since your last visit

Tola writes a short briefing of what changed since you last looked: needs you, changed, coming up. Every line links to its record; anything Tola did can be undone; anything Tola plans to do can be stopped. Earlier briefings are kept.

Reads like a good update from a colleague and suits leads who check in daily. Actions are fewer and smaller than in D1.
