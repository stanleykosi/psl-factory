Design concepts / App shell / Alerts

Where the alert bell sits and how the drawer opens

The design engineer asked for the bell in the header and leaned toward a drawer. These are four different ways to do that, so one can be chosen before any build. All four use the same six alerts, taken from the built Alerts page fixtures: the agent avatar, the sentence, the item reference, the time, and NEW while unread. All four end with the same two links, See all alerts (/notifications) and Alert settings (/settings/alerts).

ENGINEERING PROPOSAL What exists today: the desktop header (CMP-01) has the PSL FACTORY brand, the links My work, Projects, Overview, Assistant and Setup (which links show depends on the role), the person's name and role, and a sample label. The code comment says it has no Start work, bell or account menu in this release, so both the bell and Start work are added here for the first time. The phone bar has Menu, the brand and the sample label, or a back link on sub-pages. The Alerts page has filters for agent and unread, but no Mark all read, so none is drawn. The names, references and the "3 need you" split below are mock data; the fixtures do not yet say which alerts need a decision.

Concept 1: Right-side drawer

Bell with count at the right of the header. Full-height drawer over the page.

The bell sits in the header just before the person's name, with a count badge. It opens a full-height drawer about 420 px wide that slides in from the right over a dimmed page. At the top are filters for All, Unread and each agent, then the list, then the two links. Clicking a row opens the work, as the Alerts page does today. On a phone the same content becomes a full-screen sheet from the right.

Good for

  • The simplest model: one panel, the same content as the Alerts page, so there is one list to maintain.
  • Room for the agent filters people already know from the Alerts page.
  • A modal drawer has a standard keyboard pattern: focus moves in, is trapped, Esc closes it and focus goes back to the bell.

Watch out

  • It covers the page, so a person cannot read a work item and the alerts side by side.
  • It does not tell urgent from informational; a decision waits in the same list as a note.
  • At full height it is a lot of surface for three alerts.
2

Concept 2: Anchored drop panel

Bell opens a small panel hanging under it. Six items at most.

The bell opens a compact panel that hangs directly under it, with a small pointer, and does not cover the page. It shows the latest six alerts and a See all alerts link at the bottom, which is where filtering by agent lives. The panel closes when focus leaves it or on Esc. On a phone it becomes a bottom sheet with a grab handle.

Good for

  • Light: a quick look takes one click and the page stays visible.
  • Matches what most people expect from a bell.
  • Low cost to build; no scrim and no full-screen takeover on desktop.

Watch out

  • Six items is a hard cap, so older NEW alerts can sit out of sight; the count badge and the cap can disagree.
  • No room for filters, so anything beyond a glance means leaving for /notifications.
  • Small targets near the edge of a dark bar; the panel and the Start work menu could overlap if both are open.
3

Concept 3: Split bell

Bell with a “3 need you” chip. The drawer separates decisions from information.

The bell has a small red chip next to it that says how many alerts need the person, such as 3 need you. The drawer groups alerts under Needs you (decisions and exceptions) and For your information, and every row has a one-click Open that goes straight to the work. People who are only passing through can ignore the second group. On a phone it is a full-height bottom sheet with the two groups as tabs.

    Concept 4: Activity rail

    A narrow right rail that stays open and pushes the page left.

    The bell toggles a persistent rail down the right edge. When open, it sits beside the page and narrows the content instead of covering it, so a person can keep alerts in view while working. When collapsed it shrinks to a thin strip with one avatar per agent and a dot with the unread count. On a phone there is no room beside the page, so it falls back to a full-screen sheet.

    Good for

    • Alerts stay visible for people who watch the agents work all day.
    • Nothing is hidden by a scrim, and the collapsed strip shows which agent has something new.
    • No modal trap to manage on desktop: it is part of the page, so keyboard order is simple.

    Watch out

    • It takes 300 px of the page, which hurts tables and the work item page below about 1280 px.
    • The strip is permanent chrome that every role sees, including people who rarely get alerts.
    • Two different behaviours (rail on desktop, sheet on phone) must be built and tested.
    RECOMMENDATION

    My recommendation: Concept 3, built on the Concept 1 drawer

    This is a recommendation for the design engineer to accept or change, not a decision. Use the right-side drawer from Concept 1 as the shell and add the Needs you / For your information split and the chip from Concept 3.

    • Fits the product. The factory is built around people making accountable decisions, so the first thing the drawer shows is what is waiting on them, with a one-click Open.
    • Focus and Esc. On desktop the drawer is a modal overlay: focus moves into it, is trapped inside, Esc closes it, and focus returns to the bell. The bell is a real button with aria-expanded and an accessible name that includes the count, such as "Alerts, 3 need you".
    • Keyboard reach. The bell comes just before the name in tab order, so Start work stays the last item in the header and one tab from the bell. Rows are links, and Open is the only action in each row.
    • Phone. The bell, Menu and + Start each keep a 44 px target. The bottom sheet uses tabs, each 44 px tall, and it closes with a visible Close button as well as a swipe.
    • Beside Start work. The chip is the part that costs width. Show "3 need you" from about 1080 px up, and the badge alone below it, in the same breakpoint that already hides the role.

    The open question is the rule for what needs you. Until the work item state supplies it, the drawer can ship as Concept 1 (all alerts, one list) and gain the split when the rule exists.
